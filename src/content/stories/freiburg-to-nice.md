Every great adventure starts with a bad idea that nobody talks you out of. Ours: four friends, one start in Freiburg, and a finish at the Café du Cycliste in Nice, just to drink a coffee. We could have had one at home, but where’s the fun in that?

Three of us take the train from Berlin with our bikes, a small miracle of German logistics if the bike spots haven’t been sold out by the time we book. In Freiburg we pick up the fourth, who comes from Cologne and has already told everyone he is “super fit.” We’ll see.

From there the plan is simple: ride west, then south, then keep going until France stops. We follow the canals, the Saône to Lyon, and then the Rhône, the nicest cycle path in the country, flat, well signed and almost free of cars. Nobody will have to be a hero. Heroes are for people who don’t want a second croissant.

The pace is a cool 25 km/h on a good day with good legs and good weather. That’s fast enough to feel like proper cyclists and slow enough to still talk, laugh and argue about whose turn it is at the front. The coffee stops count as training. So does lunch. Dinner is recovery, obviously.

Then comes the part we’re really looking forward to: the Rhône delta, with the Mistral at our backs. That’s the wind that blows from the north and pushes you south like a very enthusiastic friend. We know it’s sometimes fierce and unpredictable, and might also blow our hats off, but we’ll take it. Tailwind is just the universe saying “keep going, you fools.”

Meanwhile we’ve asked Cervélo whether they’d lend us bikes for the trip. It’s in the works, which is the polite way of saying “we asked and nobody has said no yet.” We’d borrow them, not keep them, so please nobody panic. We’ll treat them like newborns, bring them back clean, and maybe slightly happier than we found them. If it doesn’t work out, we’ll ride our own bikes, which we love anyway, so don’t tell them.

On day ten we roll into Nice a bit tired, a bit sunburnt and extremely smug. The bikes get leaned carefully against the wall of the Café du Cycliste, the first coffee is ordered, and for a short, glorious moment, the coffee costs more than the whole train ticket. Worth it.

After that: four days of doing absolutely nothing by the sea, before we fly or take the train home, with a tan line shaped like a jersey and stories that get better every time we tell them.
